Scene 1 · The name is a sentence

What to know

Every name has four parts:

PREFIX + LOCANT + STEM + SUFFIX

Examples: 2-methylpropane | propan-1-ol | but-2-ene

Your turn

Scene 2 · Stem: count the carbons

What to know

Longest chain decides the stem:

1 C = meth- 2 C = eth- 3 C = prop- 4 C = but-

Memory trick: Monkeys Eat Peanut Butter

Your turn

Scene 3 · Suffix: the homologous series

What to know

Alkane: C–C only = -ane

Alkene: C=C = -ene

Alcohol: –OH = -ol

Carboxylic acid: –COOH = -oic acid

Ester = alkyl (from alcohol) + alkanoate (from acid)

Your turn

Scene 4 · Prefix: branches and atoms

What to know

Branch name = carbon count + -yl: 1 C = methyl, 2 C = ethyl

Atoms: chloro-, bromo-

Prefixes go at the front, in alphabetical order

Your turn

Scene 5 · Locant: where is it?

What to know

A locant is the number of the carbon holding the branch, C=C or –OH.

Number from the end that gives the functional group the LOWEST number.

Hyphen between a number and a letter: propan-2-ol. Comma between two numbers.

Your turn

Scene 6 · Four steps to name anything

What to know

Step 1: Longest chain (with the functional group) gives the stem

Step 2: Functional group gives the suffix

Step 3: Number the chain for the lowest locant

Step 4: Name branches: prefix + locant, then write the full name

Your turn

Scene 7 · Worked examples

What to know

CH₃CH₂CH₂OH = propan-1-ol

CH₃CH(OH)CH₃ = propan-2-ol

CH₂=CHCH₂CH₃ = but-1-ene | CH₃CH=CHCH₃ = but-2-ene

CH₃CH(CH₃)CH₃ = 2-methylpropane

CH₃CH₂COOH = propanoic acid (the COOH carbon is always C1)

Your turn

Scene 8 · Exam tips and try these

What to know

Positions matter only from 3–4 carbons up: ethanol and ethene need no number.

Butane and 2-methylpropane are isomers (both C₄H₁₀).

Always count the longest chain, not just the one drawn straight.

Check your specification for the range of compounds you must name.

Your turn

Type names in lower case with hyphens, for example propan-2-ol. Formulae can be typed like CH3CH=CHCH3.
